## Scope
Wave 2, session B9 of docs/SPEC.md section 10 (feature F5), on branch `build/b9-monitoring`. B9 delivers:
- intraday checks of every open paper trade of every strategy at every horizon N+1..N+5, both views
  (accuracy and head-to-head): price vs entry, vs target and vs the trade's own range; the flags

Monitoring only: a check never trades, never predicts and never recommends. The schedules are unchanged: India
11:13 and 14:13 IST, US 12:27 and 14:57 New York time (docs/DESIGN.md section 2).

## Contract
### What counts as an open trade (as of the check time `check_at`)
- Accuracy view: each `strategy_predictions` row with `qualifies` true and direction `up` whose window
  `session_date` (D) <= today <= `exit_date` holds. trade_id = `acc:<prediction_id>`.
- Head-to-head view: each `head_to_head_picks` row with status `picked` whose prediction is such a row.
  trade_id = `h2h:<pick_rule>:<prediction_id>`.
- Only rows stored by `check_at` count; per id the first stored row wins (ids are skipped once they exist).
- A prediction or pick made at or after D's open is refused, as the settlement refuses it (F1.8). The run
  summary counts it under `skipped_trades.not_locked`.
- India: a trade whose single share costs more than its amount is not a trade (F1.4). It is listed under
  `skipped_trades.price_above_amount` and gets no row.
- An open trade on a ticker that is not on the market's ticker list gets rows with quality `no_quote` and the
  note `not_on_watchlist`. It is also listed under `skipped_trades.not_on_watchlist`.
- The window ends at the stored `exit_date`. A trade still unsettled after it (`exit_delayed`) is not checked
  again.

### `trade_checks` (W1's kind, `core/schema_lab.py`, unchanged)
There is one row per open trade per check. The id is `<check_id>-<trade_id>` and `check_row_id` is
`<check_id>-<ticker>`, the ticker's `intraday_checks` row. Each row is written to the day file of check_at's UTC
date, with `method_version` `tc-v1`.

Stored as predicted (raw): `entry_price` (D's raw open), `target_price`, `lo80`, `lo50`, `hi50` and `hi80`.

Measured against `last_price`, the newest complete 5-minute bar by the check. These are all on today's price
basis (see `basis_factor` below):
- `ret_since_entry_pct` = (last / entry_adj - 1) x 100.
- `to_target_pct` = (target_adj / last - 1) x 100. Negative means the price is already past the target.
- `band`: below80 | below50 | inside50 | above50 | above80, against the adjusted range. A price exactly on an
  edge is inside that band, as in WS5.
- `target_z` = (target_adj / last - 1) / (sigma_1d x sqrt(sessions_left)).
- `session_number`: 1 = D.
- `flags`: these three flags all mark `flagged` (`trade_flag_on`):
  - `outside_range`: band below80 or above80.
  - `far_from_target`: the target is not reached yet and `target_z` >= `thresholds.target_z` (2.0).
  - `against_prediction`: z_since_entry <= -`thresholds.against_call_z` (1.0). This is WS5's `against_call`
    rule applied to the move since the trade's entry.

### `trade_check_details` (new, B9, `core/schema_intraday.py`)
There is one row per `trade_checks` row, with the same id. It holds the measures W1's columns lack:
- `quality`: ok | stale_quote | no_quote | no_entry_price. Any value other than ok means no measures and no
  flags: `last_price`, `band` and `target_z` stay null in `trade_checks`.
- `entry_source`: intraday_open (D is today: the first 5-minute open) | stored_open (D's stored raw open).
- `basis_factor`: the split/bonus factors with an ex-date after the prediction's as-of date, using only
  adjustments detected by `check_at`.
- `entry_adj`, `target_adj`, `lo80_adj`..`hi80_adj`: the trade's prices on today's basis.
- `target_reached` and `target_reached_session` (F1.9 definition, so far). A session high from D up to the
  check reaches the adjusted target. The sessions before today come from stored bars, and today from complete
  5-minute bars only. The value is null when the target was not reached before today and today's quote is
  stale or missing.
- `high_since_entry_pct` and `low_since_entry_pct`.
- `sigma_1d`, `elapsed_fraction`, `sessions_held` (= session_number - 1 + elapsed), `sessions_left` (sessions to
  the exit close, the rest of today included, never below `min_elapsed_fraction`) and `z_since_entry`.
- `notes`: `no_bar_<date>`, `no_sigma`, `not_on_watchlist`.

### `intraday_checks` and `intraday_runs` (additive)
- `intraday_checks.bands` is a JSON object `{"<k>": {range_id, lo80, lo50, hi50, hi80, sigma_h, band}}` with
  one entry for every horizon k that has a published range for the session.
  - The WS5 columns `_1d` and `_5d` still repeat k = 1 and k = 5.
  - `intraday_checks.open_trades` is the number of the ticker's open trades.
  - The new flag `open_trade_flagged` is a trigger: at least one of the ticker's open trades is flagged.
    The ticker row then gets attribution candidates, so the explainer can cover it.
  - Band flags exist for every horizon k: `outside_<k>d_80`, plus `outside_<k>d_50` for the shortest horizon.
    The WS5 names are the cases k = 1 and k = 5.
  - In `flag_on`, the template `outside_{k}d_80` stands for the 80% flag of every horizon.
- `intraday_runs` gains `open_trades`, `trades_flagged` and `alerts` (counts).

### The alerts feed: `intraday_alerts` (new, B9, `core/schema_intraday.py`), for B6
Every check appends its alerts to `data/<market>/intraday_alerts/` (day file of check_at's UTC date). The alerts
are deterministic, built from the check's own rows and the news stored by `check_at`. Each record:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| id | `<check_id>-<ticker>-trades` or `<check_id>-<ticker>-news-<news_id>` |
| check_id, check_at, session_date, market, ticker | the check and the company |
| alert_type | `open_trade_flagged` or `material_news_open_trade` |
| check_row_id | the ticker's `intraday_checks` row; the explainer's note joins on it |
| trade_ids | flagged trades (trade alert), or every open trade of the ticker (news alert) |
| trades | JSON list, trade alert only: `[{trade_id, strategy_id, view, horizon_days, flags, band, ret_since_entry_pct, to_target_pct, target_reached}]` |
| flags | the union of the trades' flags (trade alert); `[]` for news |
| repeat | trade alert: true when every listed trade was already alerted with the same flags earlier this session; news: false |
| news_id, news_title, news_source, news_status, news_materiality, news_first_seen_at | news alert only. The status (DESIGN.md 3b) and materiality (news-analyst enrichment) are as of check_at |
| method_version, computed_at | `tc-v1`, the run time |

The two alert types:
- **Trade alert:** one row per ticker that has at least one flagged open trade.
- **News alert:** one row per news item tagged with a ticker that has an open trade. The item must be first
  seen since the previous session's close and published by the check. Its enrichment by the check must have
  a materiality in `alerts.news_materiality` (`[high]`). Every such item is read, with no count limit before the
  materiality filter. Each item is alerted once per session and ticker.

Reading the feed:
- The view `intraday_alerts_feed` adds the explainer's note on the check row (`explanation_id`, `attribution`,
  `explanation`, `cited_ids`, `explained_at`). B6 filters on `explained_at` for a time.
- `python scripts/intraday_check.py alerts [--check ID]` prints one check's alerts as JSON.
- The feed never says buy or sell. B6 decides what to post: for example, a trade alert with `repeat` false,
  and every news alert.

### Views (`sql/views.sql`, B9 block at the end)
- `trade_check_rows`: each trade check joined with its details row (first stored row per id).
- `trade_checks_latest`: the newest check per trade.
- `intraday_alerts_feed`: described above.
- `intraday_explanation_close` also carries `bands`. `outcomes.py` adds `closed_outside_80`
  `{"<k>": bool}` for every horizon, and keeps `closed_outside_1d_80`.

### Explainer input
- `intraday_check.py prepare` adds `trades` to each flagged row: that row's flagged trade checks, with their
  measures.
- The note stays one per flagged ticker row, at most 60 words, citing only the row's candidates.
- The gate also accepts these numbers:
  - each trade's stored numbers: entry, last, target and range edges (raw and adjusted),
    `ret_since_entry_pct` and `to_target_pct` (signed %), `target_z` and `z_since_entry` (signed), and the
    session number;
  - the last price's % distance from each of the trade's edges;
  - the band edges of every published horizon;
  - any horizon the row knows (from the bands, calls, trades, or `config/strategies.yaml`).
- Trade and strategy ids in the text are skipped like the other ids. The forbidden words are unchanged, so a
  note cannot say "target".

### Horizons
- No horizon is fixed in B9's logic: flags, bands, sigma, the call lookback, the gate's horizon numbers, the
  outcomes and the payload all follow the horizons published or configured. The only remaining 1 and 5 are
  WS5's stored legacy columns:
  - `rows.py` `LEGACY_HORIZONS` fills `range_id_1d`..`band_5d` for k = 1 and 5, because those columns exist in
    the schema and in older rows;
  - `outcomes.py` keeps the `closed_outside_1d_80` key beside `closed_outside_80`;
  - the gate reads older rows' `band_<k>d` columns when a row has no `bands`;
  - `CALL_LOOKBACK_DAYS = 12` stays the minimum lookback.
- The horizon list comes from `contracts.horizons.horizons()` once B10 builds it. Until then it comes from
  `config/strategies.yaml` `horizons`. Each row's bands are the horizons published for the session.
- The 1-day sigma is the shortest published horizon's `sigma_h / sqrt(k)`, and the EWMA vol otherwise.
- The call lookback grows with the longest horizon.
- The fixtures use W1's example open trades, predictions, head-to-head picks and per-horizon ranges until B2
  and B10 merge. Nothing B9 reads needs a change when their real rows arrive, because the column names are
  W1's.

## Open questions
Each question has the default B9 took.
1. **Where "target reached" and the trade's quality are stored.** W1's `trade_checks` has no column for them,
   and its column list is pinned by W1's catalogue test.
   - Default: a companion kind `trade_check_details` (B9's), joined by id in the view `trade_check_rows`.
   - The orchestrator may later fold the columns into `trade_checks` together with the catalogue.
2. **`far_from_target` once the target was reached.** The flag is raised only while the target has not been
   reached. A trade that touched its target and fell back is never flagged far from it.
   - Default: as above, following F1.9's "reached" (any high in the window).
3. **A trade bought today** (D = today) uses the first 5-minute bar's open as its entry. The official open
   comes with the next stored bar.
   - Default: as above; the row's `entry_source` says `intraday_open`.
4. **Which news is "material".** News is material when the news-analyst's enrichment, as of the check, says
   `high`. Items first seen since the pre-open enrichment are usually not enriched yet, so their alert waits
   for an enrichment. NSE announcements carry no materiality and give no alerts.
   - Default: `alerts.news_materiality: [high]`. B6 or the owner may add `medium` or a status rule.
5. **More ticker rows get flagged** once B10 publishes ranges for horizons 2-4. The 80% flag of every
   horizon is now a trigger (`outside_{k}d_80`); before, only 1d and 5d were.
   - Default: every horizon. The owner may list single horizons in `flag_on` instead.
6. **Trades past their exit date** (`exit_delayed`, no exit close yet) are not monitored after `exit_date`.
   - Default: not monitored; the settlement handles them.
